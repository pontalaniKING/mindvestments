Go outside within thirty minutes of waking and stay there for about ten minutes. No sunglasses, no window in between. That is the whole thing.

## Why this works

Your sleep timing is set by a clock in the brain that runs slightly long on its own. Light in the first part of your day is the main signal that resets it, and the reset works forwards: morning light pulls your whole sleep window earlier, including the time you get tired at night.

The size of the signal matters more than people expect. A bright room is somewhere around 100 to 300 lux. An overcast morning outside is around 10,000. Glass blocks much of the useful part, which is why sitting by a window is a much weaker version of the same idea.

This is also why it often fixes a problem that looks like something else. If you cannot fall asleep until one in the morning and then feel wrecked all afternoon, the usual explanation is not poor sleep quality. It is a clock with nothing steady to lock on to.

## How to do it

1. Pick the ten minutes you already spend doing something else. Coffee outside, the walk to the station, the dog, the bins.
2. Go out within half an hour of waking, before anything else negotiates you out of it.
3. Do not wear sunglasses. Do not look at the sun.
4. Do it every day, including weekends. The weekend is usually where the drift happens.
5. Pair it with a fixed wake time. Light and wake time work together, and neither does much alone.

## Common mistakes

- **Doing it through a window.** Much weaker. Open the door and stand in it if you have to.
- **Waiting for sun.** Cloud is fine. Rain is fine. It is still an order of magnitude brighter than indoors.
- **Only on weekdays.** A two-hour weekend lie-in undoes most of a week's work.
- **Expecting it tonight.** The clock moves over days. Give it a fortnight before judging it.
- **Dark winter mornings.** Above roughly 55° north there are months where there is nothing to get. A 10,000 lux lamp at the breakfast table for twenty to thirty minutes is the substitute, not an upgrade.

## What should change

Within a week or two, falling asleep should start to happen earlier without you trying, and the afternoon dip should get shallower. If neither moves at all after three weeks, your limiting factor is somewhere else — look at caffeine timing or wake time consistency next.

## Your next step

Once the morning is fixed, the evening is the other half of the same lever. Cutting caffeine earlier is usually the bigger of the two.
